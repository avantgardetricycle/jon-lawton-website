The latest · 12th album

Midnight with the Light On

Two sessions make up the record. One side leans country, the other brings in horns, background singers, and a killer rhythm section. It's the clearest picture yet of everywhere Jon's music has been.

The record was cut in two different rooms: one at Coyote Road Studios in Santa Barbara with engineer Sean McCue, the other at Greaseland Studio in San Jose with Kid Andersen. That split is a good part of why it moves the way it does, country warmth on one side and blues-gospel grit on the other.

From Jon

Why any of this exists

Jon started writing songs at fourteen, and by his own account he's never really stopped since. He describes these recordings less as a catalog than as a kind of time machine: hearing one back brings the whole session rushing back, the musicians, the jokes, the disagreements, the explanations. A few of these songs were only ever played once, live, and happened to get caught on tape, which he still can't quite believe his luck on.

Most of it gets written the way songs get written for a lot of people who drive as much as he does: groggily, in the middle of the night, on whatever paper happens to be around, or worked out to the rhythm of tires on a quiet road. He's never been the type to sit down and write a song as an exercise, which is maybe why it still catches him off guard when other people manage to pull that off well.

He'll be the first to admit the business side of music has mostly passed him by. He's a few steps behind on merch and marketing, by his own estimate. What's here isn't a vanity project; it's closer to a pile of buried treasure he's hoping the right stranger eventually stumbles onto. He counts Steve Earle, John Prine, Muddy Waters, Robert Johnson, Neil Young, Joni Mitchell, Merle Haggard, CSNY, Townes van Zandt, Lucinda Williams, and Dylan among the songwriters that keep him honest.

As he puts it, he still wants to “push these songs through the birth canal” and get them out where people can actually hear them.
